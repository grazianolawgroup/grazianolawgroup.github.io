Common Questions

Estate Planning FAQ

Do I need a will if I don't have many assets?

A will does more than distribute property — it names guardians for minor children and designates who handles your affairs. Most adults benefit from having at least a basic will regardless of estate size.

What's the difference between a will and a trust?

A will directs how assets are distributed after death and typically goes through probate. A trust can hold and manage assets during your lifetime and after, and may avoid probate depending on how it's structured. Many estate plans use both.

Can I update my estate plan later?

Yes. Estate plans should be reviewed after major life events — marriage, divorce, a new child, buying property, or the death of a named beneficiary or fiduciary.

Does Florida recognize wills executed in another state?

Florida generally recognizes out-of-state wills that were validly executed where they were signed, but Florida has specific execution and witness requirements. It's worth having an out-of-state estate plan reviewed after relocating to Florida.
